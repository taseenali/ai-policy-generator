## CLOSER LOOK 21.1 — ACID RAIN

### Definition
- Acid rain is precipitation with pH below 5.6 (normal rain pH ~5.6 from CO₂ dissolving to form carbonic acid)
- Wet deposition: rain, snow, fog
- Dry deposition: sulfate and nitrate particles deposited directly; later activated by moisture to become acids

### Formation
- SO₂ and NOₓ emitted from fossil fuel burning → react with O₂ and H₂O vapor in atmosphere → form sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃)
- SO₂ primarily from stationary sources (coal power plants)
- NOₓ from both stationary and mobile sources (automobiles)
- Can travel long distances with prevailing winds before deposition

### Sensitivity Factors
- Sensitive areas: granite bedrock or sandy soils — cannot buffer acid input
- Resistant areas: limestone bedrock (CaCO₃ = calcite) — neutralizes acid; called "hard water lakes" when lakes are on such rocks

### Effects on Forest Ecosystems
- Does NOT directly kill trees; weakens them:
  1. Essential nutrients leached from soils
  2. Nutrients stripped from leaves by acid fog
  3. Releases toxic chemicals (e.g., aluminum) that damage trees
- Bavaria (German evergreen trees died), Appalachian/Vermont (50% red spruce died)

### Effects on Lake Ecosystems (three mechanisms)
1. Damages aquatic species directly — disrupts life processes; e.g., crayfish produce fewer eggs, malformed larvae
2. Dissolves necessary chemical elements → leaves lake with outflow → elements lost (no longer cycle in lake)
3. Leaches metals (Al, Pb, Hg, Ca) from soils/rocks → discharges into rivers/lakes
   - Aluminum clogs fish gills → suffocation
   - Heavy metals biomagnify → pass to people, mammals, birds
   - Drinking water from acidic lakes may have high toxic metal concentrations

### Effects on Human Society
- Damages building materials: steel, paint, plastics, cement, masonry, limestone, sandstone, marble
- Stone decays ~2× faster in cities than rural areas (acid rain + humidity + corrosive groundwater)

## CLOSER LOOK 21.2 — HIGH-ALTITUDE (STRATOSPHERIC) OZONE DEPLETION

### Ozone (O₃) — Stratospheric vs. Tropospheric
- ~90% of atmospheric ozone is in stratosphere (15–40 km altitude; peak ~400 ppb at 25–30 km)
- Stratospheric ozone = "ozone shield" — protects Earth from UV radiation
- Tropospheric ozone = pollutant (photochemical smog)

### UV Radiation Types
| Type | Wavelength | Energy | Fate |
|------|-----------|--------|------|
| UVC | Shortest | Most energetic | Completely absorbed in stratosphere; breaks O₂ → ozone formation |
| UVB | Medium | Energetic | Strongly absorbed by ozone (only gas that absorbs UVB); depletion = more UVB at surface = HAZARD |
| UVA | Longest | Least energetic | Not affected by ozone; transmitted to surface; causes some cell damage |

### Natural Ozone Formation (Chapman cycle)
1. UVC breaks O₂ → O + O (photodissociation)
2. O + O₂ → O₃ (ozone formation)
3. O₃ + UVC → O₂ + O (ozone destruction — natural balance)

### CFCs and Ozone Depletion — Molina-Rowland Hypothesis
**Three-step process**:
1. CFCs emitted in lower atmosphere → very stable, nonreactive → long residence time (~100 years)
2. CFCs disperse upward into stratosphere (no tropospheric sinks except possibly soils)
3. In upper stratosphere, UVC breaks down CFCs → releases chlorine (highly reactive atom)

**Catalytic chain reaction**:
- Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂
- ClO + O → Cl + O₂
- Net: O₃ + O → 2O₂ (ozone destroyed, chlorine regenerated!)
- Each Cl atom destroys ~100,000 ozone molecules over 1–2 years before removal

**Interruption of chain**:
- Cl + CH₄ → HCl (hydrochloric acid) — storage/buffer; can diffuse downward
- ClO + NO₂ → ClONO₂ (chlorine nitrate) — temporary reservoir
- HCl in troposphere → removed by rain (ultimate sink)

### Ozone Measurements
- Dobson Unit (DU): 1 DU = 1 ppb O₃
- Antarctic ozone: ~300 DU (1957–1970) → dropped to ~140 DU by 1986 ("ozone hole")
- Measured in October (Antarctic spring)

### Environmental Effects
- Increased UVB reaches surface
- Human: skin cancers, cataracts, immune system suppression
- Food chains damaged (land and ocean)
- Protection: limit sun 10am–4pm, sunscreen SPF ≥30, UV-protective sunglasses, avoid tanning salons, consult UV Index

### Policy Response
- US banned CFCs as propellants in 1978
- Montreal Protocol (1987): 24 countries signed to reduce/eliminate CFC production

### Other Ozone-Depleting Substances
- N₂O (nitrous oxide) — also a GHG
- Methyl bromide (CH₃Br) — agricultural fumigant
- Halons — fire extinguishers
