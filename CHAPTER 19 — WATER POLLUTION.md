## 2. DEFINITIONS

[Concept ID: ch19-001] Water pollution
- Definition: Degradation of water quality to the point that traditional uses and/or desirable life forms are threatened. Based on intended use, departure from norm, effects on public health, or ecological impacts. Pollutants can be physical (sediment), chemical (PCBs), or biological (viruses, bacteria).
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slide 3]

[Concept ID: ch19-002] Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)
- Definition: Amount of oxygen consumed by microorganisms in water in a fixed amount of time (typically 5 days — BOD₅). Caused by respiration of bacteria consuming organic matter, so BOD is proportional to organic matter. Measured routinely at wastewater treatment plants. ~33% of all BOD in streams from agricultural activities.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, MCQ, numerical thresholds
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slide 12]

[Concept ID: ch19-003] Dissolved Oxygen (DO) threshold
- Definition: EPA defines pollution alert threshold at DO levels below 5 mg/L. High BOD causes low DO, leading to fish kills.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: MCQ, numerical
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slide 14]

[Concept ID: ch19-004] BOD zones (three)
- Definition: After polluting event: (1) Pollution zone — high BOD; (2) Active decomposition zone — DO reaches minimum; (3) Recovery zone — DO increases, BOD reduced.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: list, order, MCQ, diagram
- Source: [ch19_2.pdf, slide 15]

[Concept ID: ch19-005] Eutrophication
- Definition: Process by which a body of water develops a high concentration of nutrients (N, P). Nutrients increase growth of aquatic plants, algae, and photosynthetic bacteria. When these die and decompose, BOD increases, reducing DO, sometimes killing fish and other organisms. This is an ecosystem effect (not direct poisoning).
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, process steps, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slide 18]

[Concept ID: ch19-006] Cultural eutrophication
- Definition: Human-accelerated process of adding nutrients to water. Solution: prevent excessive nutrient loadings to water.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, distinguish from natural, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slide 21]

[Concept ID: ch19-007] Oligotrophic lake
- Definition: Lake with relatively low concentration of nutrients; clear water; low abundance of aquatic life.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, compare with eutrophic, MCQ
- Source: [ch19_2.pdf, slide 19]

[Concept ID: ch19-008] Eutrophic lake
- Definition: Lake with high concentration of nutrients; often with mats of algae and murky water; abundance of life.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, compare, MCQ
- Source: [ch19_2.pdf, slide 19]

[Concept ID: ch19-009] Fecal coliform bacteria
- Definition: Bacteria used as indicator of waterborne disease. Presence indicates fecal matter contamination. Includes Escherichia coli (E. coli). Although generally harmless, their presence suggests disease organisms may be present. EPA thresholds: 200 cells/100ml for swimming; 0 cells/100ml for drinking water.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, thresholds, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.2; ch19_2.pdf, slide 24]

[Concept ID: ch19-010] Waterborne disease
- Definition: Disease transmitted via water contaminated with fecal matter; also called fecal-oral or "toilet to mouth" disease. Primary water-pollution problem in the world today. Examples: cholera, Cryptosporidium (1993 Milwaukee outbreak — 400,000 people affected).
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, MCQ, case examples
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.2; ch19_2.pdf, slides 3, 23]

[Concept ID: ch19-011] Acid mine drainage
- Definition: Water with high concentration of sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) draining from mines (especially coal). Pyrite (FeS₂) contacts oxygen and water → sulfuric acid. Acidic water toxic to aquatic ecosystem; damages biological productivity; fish die. Can also pollute groundwater.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, chemical reaction, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.2; ch19_2.pdf, slides 31–33]

[Concept ID: ch19-012] Point source pollutant
- Definition: Distinct and confined sites that empty into streams or rivers (e.g., pipes from municipal/industrial sites). Easier to monitor and control. Regulated by permit.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, compare, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.3; ch19_2.pdf, slide 36]

[Concept ID: ch19-013] Nonpoint source pollutant
- Definition: Diffused and intermittent sources (e.g., runoff). Influenced by land use, climate, hydrology, topography, native vegetation, geology. Difficult to monitor and control.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, compare, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.3; ch19_2.pdf, slide 37]

[Concept ID: ch19-014] Primary treatment (wastewater)
- Definition: Raw sewage enters plant → screens remove large floating organic material → grit chamber (sand, small stones, grit removed) → sedimentation tank (particulate matter settles out as sludge) → sludge transported to digester. Removes ~35% of BOD.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, steps, percentages, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.4; ch19_2.pdf, slides 51–53]

[Concept ID: ch19-015] Secondary treatment (activated sludge)
- Definition: Wastewater from primary sedimentation tank → aeration tank (mixed with air and some sludge — contains aerobic bacteria that consume BOD) → final sedimentation tank (sludge settles out) → sludge reused/digested. Removes ~90% of BOD. Wastewater disinfected with chlorine and discharged.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, steps, percentages, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.4; ch19_2.pdf, slides 52, 55]

[Concept ID: ch19-016] Advanced wastewater treatment
- Definition: Additional treatment steps beyond secondary: sand filters, carbon filters, chemicals applied to remove specific pollutants. Treated water used for agricultural/municipal irrigation (golf courses, parks).
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, give examples, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.4; ch19_2.pdf, slide 57]

[Concept ID: ch19-017] Septic tank system
- Definition: Home waste treatment: sewer line → underground tank → solids separate and settle; clarified liquid → absorption (drain) field → soil filtration. Requires well-drained soils, proper sizing, periodic pumping. Failure causes: not pumping full tank, poor soil drainage.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, explain, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.3; ch19_2.pdf, slides 47–49]

[Concept ID: ch19-018] Water reuse — types
- Definition: (1) Inadvertent reuse — water withdrawn, used, treated, returned to source; subsequent use downstream. (2) Indirect reuse — deliberate plan; wastewater treated and recycled back into source. (3) Direct reuse — treated wastewater piped directly to next user (mostly non-potable: golf courses, etc.).
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, distinguish, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.5]

[Concept ID: ch19-019] Gray water
- Definition: Domestic wastewater from laundry and sinks (separate from black water — toilets). Can be used for watering lawns or washing cars.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.5]

[Concept ID: ch19-020] Groundwater pollution
- Definition: ~½ of US population depends on groundwater for drinking. Low turnover time makes pollution difficult to rectify. 175,000 US waste disposal sites suspected of leaking contaminants. Hazard depends on concentration, toxicity, and exposure.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, statistics, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.3; ch19_2.pdf, slide 41]

[Concept ID: ch19-021] Chlorine treatment
- Definition: Effective in killing pathogens to prevent waterborne disease. However, chlorine treatment by-products may pose hazard to fish and cancer risk to humans (degree of risk controversial). Shock chlorination followed by chlorine removal practiced to minimize residues.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, pros/cons, MCQ
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.4; ch19_2.pdf, slide 58]

## 4. PROCESSES & CAUSE-EFFECT CHAINS

### 4.1 BOD and dissolved oxygen relationship
[Concept ID: ch19-032]
Organic matter input → bacteria multiply and respire → consume O₂ → BOD increases → DO decreases → below 5 mg/L = pollution alert → fish kills possible
Three zones downstream from pollution source: Pollution zone → Active decomposition zone → Recovery zone
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slides 15–16]

### 4.2 Eutrophication chain
[Concept ID: ch19-033]
Nutrient loading (N, P) → algal/bacterial bloom → dense layer forms → bottom algae die → bacteria decompose dead algae → consume O₂ → DO drops → fish and aquatic organisms die (ecosystem effect)
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.1; ch19_2.pdf, slide 22]

### 4.3 Acid mine drainage formation
[Concept ID: ch19-034]
Pyrite (FeS₂) + Oxygen (O₂) + Water (H₂O) → Chemical weathering → Sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) → Acidic water drains into streams/lakes/groundwater → Toxic to plants and animals → damages biological productivity → fish death
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch19 Summary.pdf, p.2; ch19_2.pdf, slides 31–33]

### 4.4 Wastewater treatment plant process
[Concept ID: ch19-035]
1. Raw sewage enters from municipal sewer line
2. Screens → remove large floating organic material
3. Grit chamber → sand, small stones, grit removed
4. Primary sedimentation tank → particulate matter settles → sludge
5. Aeration tank → mixed with air and activated sludge → aerobic bacteria consume BOD
6. Final sedimentation tank → sludge settles out
7. Chlorination → disinfection
8. Discharge to surface water
Optionally: Digester → anaerobic bacteria → produce methane (used as fuel)
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch19_2.pdf, slides 52–55]

## 8. CASE EXAMPLES

[Concept ID: ch19-041] **Hudson River**: America's "First River"; polluted to point of fishing/swimming bans; Superfund site; recent court decisions → attempted cleanup; counted as victory for water cleanup and environmental justice.

[Concept ID: ch19-042] **Exxon Valdez (March 24, 1989)**: Prince William Sound, Alaska; 11 million gallons; 250,000 barrels; killed 13% harbor seals, 28% otters, 100,000–645,000 seabirds; only ~14% recovered; oil biodegradable but long-term effects unknown.

[Concept ID: ch19-043] **Deepwater Horizon (2010)**: Gulf of Mexico; largest oil spill ever at 205 million gallons; effects still being evaluated.

[Concept ID: ch19-044] **Potomac River**: Once so polluted dangerous to swim in; greatly improved by pollution control of point sources.

[Concept ID: ch19-045] **Cuyahoga River**: Cleveland; ignited in flames in 1969; today cleaner and no longer flammable.

[Concept ID: ch19-046] **Milwaukee Cryptosporidium outbreak (1993)**: 400,000 people affected; shows even developed countries vulnerable.

[Concept ID: ch19-047] **Cholera outbreak London (1854)**: Traced to public well on Broad Street; established importance of waterborne disease.

[Concept ID: ch19-048] **Catskills water supply**: NY City; natural ecosystem filtering; one of largest municipal water supplies in US that didn't require extensive filtering.

[Concept ID: ch19-049] **Netherlands wastewater treatment**: Wastewater pumped into sand dunes; natural filtering and biological treatment; water recycled back into groundwater for human consumption.

[Concept ID: ch19-050] **Rural China**: Waste used to generate methane for cooking; recycled back onto agricultural fields.

[Concept ID: ch19-051] **New Haven, CT**: After rainstorm, undigested corn from sewage treatment plant visible on mud flats in harbor.

## 9. COMMON EXAM TRAPS
[Concept ID: ch19-052]
1. **DO threshold for pollution alert is 5 mg/L** (not 10 or 3).
2. **Eutrophication kills fish through O₂ depletion, NOT direct toxicity of nutrients.** This is an ecosystem effect.
3. **Fecal coliforms are generally harmless but indicate fecal contamination.** They're an indicator, not the disease agent itself.
4. **BOD is a measure of oxygen consumed by microorganisms, NOT a measure of pollutants directly.**
5. **Groundwater has LOW turnover time** (makes pollution hard to fix).
6. **Sediment is the greatest water pollutant by volume and mass** (not chemicals or oil).
7. **Primary treatment removes ~35% BOD; secondary removes ~90% BOD** (not the other way around).
