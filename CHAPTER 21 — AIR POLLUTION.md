[Concept ID: ch21-001] Primary pollutant

[Concept ID: ch21-003] Stationary sources
- Definition: Air pollution sources with relatively fixed location. Three types: (1) Point sources — smokestacks, power plants, controllable sites; (2) Fugitive sources — open areas exposed to wind (construction, agricultural burning, mines); (3) Area sources — well-defined areas with multiple sources (urban communities, industries, agricultural sprays).

- Recall angles: define, classify, MCQ

[Concept ID: ch21-004] Mobile sources

- Recall angles: define, MCQ

[Concept ID: ch21-005] Synergistic effect

[Concept ID: ch21-008] Sulfurous smog (London-type / gray air / industrial smog)

- Recall angles: define, compare, MCQ

[Concept ID: ch21-009] Atmospheric inversion
- Definition: Occurs when warmer air is found above cooler air; restricts circulation in lower atmosphere; may lead to severe pollution events. Two types: (1) summer/fall radiation inversion, (2) cloud cover with stagnant air.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, explain, MCQ
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.49–50]

[Concept ID: ch21-010] Global dimming
- Definition: Gradual reduction in solar energy reaching Earth's surface due to particulate matter; cools the atmosphere; lessens global warming.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, MCQ
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.31; Activities.md Ch 21 q11]

[Concept ID: ch21-011] Air toxics
- Definition: Pollutants known or suspected to cause cancer or other serious health problems (long-term or short-term exposure). Include gases, metals, and organic chemicals emitted in relatively small volumes. Standards set for more than 150 air toxics. EPA estimates average cancer risk from air toxics ~1 in 21,000.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, list, MCQ
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.33–34]

[Concept ID: ch21-012] Air Quality Index (AQI)
- Definition: Index describing air pollution on a given day; determined from five major pollutants (PM, SO₂, CO, O₃, NO₂). AQI=100: unhealthy; >200: alert; >300: warning (hazardous to all); >400: emergency.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, thresholds, MCQ
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.66–67]

[Concept ID: ch21-013] Chimney effect (stack effect)
- Definition: Occurs when temperature differential between indoor and outdoor environments causes warmer air to rise in building to upper levels, replaced in lower portion by outdoor air. Can draw pollutants (e.g., secondhand smoke) into building.
- Must memorize: yes
- Recall angles: define, explain, MCQ
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.78]

[Concept ID: ch21-014] Sick building syndrome (SBS)
- Definition: Condition where people report symptoms (headaches, skin irritation, memory loss, chronic fatigue, allergy/asthma) that cannot be traced to any one known cause.

- Recall angles: define, distinguish from BRI, MCQ

[Concept ID: ch21-015] Building-related illnesses (BRIs)

## 4. PROCESSES & CAUSE-EFFECT CHAINS

### 4.1 Photochemical smog formation
[Concept ID: ch21-029]
NOₓ + VOC + Sunlight → Photochemical reactions → O₃ (ozone) + PANs (peroxyacyl nitrates) → Photochemical smog (LA-type/brown air)
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.23–25]

### 4.2 Atmospheric inversion and pollution
[Concept ID: ch21-030]
Warmer air above cooler air → restricts vertical air circulation → pollutants trapped near surface → severe pollution events. Worse in cities in valleys/topographic bowls (e.g., Los Angeles, Mexico City).
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.49–51]

### 4.3 Radon gas pathway
[Concept ID: ch21-031]
Uranium in rocks/soil → radioactive decay chain → Radium-226 → Radon-222 (half-life 3.8 days) → alpha decay to Polonium-218 (half-life ~3 min) → enters buildings through: (1) migration from soil/rock into basements; (2) dissolved in groundwater pumped into homes; (3) radon-contaminated building materials.
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.82–84]

### 4.4 Chimney effect
[Concept ID: ch21-032]
Temperature difference indoor/outdoor → warmer indoor air rises → upper levels → outdoor air drawn into lower portion → pollutants (e.g., secondhand smoke) drawn into building from outside.
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.78]

## 5. COMPARISONS

### 5.1 Primary vs. Secondary pollutants
[Concept ID: ch21-033]
| Aspect | Primary | Secondary |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| Definition | Emitted directly into air | Formed through reactions in atmosphere |
| Examples | CO, VOCs, NOₓ, SOₓ, particulates, hydrocarbons | O₃ (ozone), PANs |
| Control | Reduce emissions at source | More difficult — must control precursors |
- Must memorize: yes
- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.12]

[Concept ID: ch21-034]

|--------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| Primary cause | Automobiles (NOₓ + sunlight) | Coal/oil combustion |
| Color | Brown air | Gray air |
| Also called | LA-type smog | Industrial smog |

- Source: [ch21.pdf, p.46]

### 5.3 Sick building syndrome vs. Building-related illness
[Concept ID: ch21-035]

|--------|-----|-----|
